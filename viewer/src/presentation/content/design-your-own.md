## Design your own module

The film back works with any body that keeps to the interface above, and any body can take a module with an M42 thread. These are the limits to design inside, followed by some directions that have not been tried.

### Limits

- **Cover the frame.** Lenses made for 135 film cameras cover a frame {{value:standard_frame_width}} wide, so most of them fall short of the {{value:image_circle}} this camera needs. Enlarger and medium format lenses are the likelier fit. None has been tested here yet.
- **Set the distance.** A lens focuses at one distance from the film. The simple body puts the mount {{value:m42_seat_to_film}} from it, and lenses made for SLR cameras need a shorter distance, so a lens needs its own body.
- **Touch only the datum.** A body rests on the datum face, the hook rail and the latch pin. It stays clear of the chambers on either side of the gate.
- **Keep the light out.** Line the chamber with black and keep a velvet seal at the datum face.

### Directions

- **A body with a built-in shutter.** The pinhole module carries its own sliding shutter. A body could take that job over and leave the module a bare pinhole or lens.
- **A reflex or waist-level body.** A mirror and a ground glass in the body would let you frame through the lens. It uses the same hook and latch.
- **A lens module.** A fixed-focus or zone-focus barrel on the M42 thread, with an iris from a bought M42 part.
