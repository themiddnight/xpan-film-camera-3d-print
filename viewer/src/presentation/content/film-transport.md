## How the film moves

One turn of the advance knob moves exactly one frame. The take-up spool pulls the film across the gate, and a sprocket with {{number:sprocket_teeth}} teeth rides in the perforations. A frame is {{number:perfs_per_frame}} perforations long, so after one full turn of the sprocket, {{value:advance_per_frame}} of film has gone by and a spring-loaded pawl drops into a notch on a cam. The sprocket locks with a click and you stop turning.

{{figure:transport-cutaway}}

The cam and the sprocket are one printed part on a fixed steel pin, so the notch can never slip out of step with the teeth. Pressing the release button lifts the pawl out of the notch and the sprocket turns freely. Once the cam has passed under the pawl you can let go, and the pawl rides the edge of the cam until the notch comes round again.

The film wraps the sprocket far enough to engage only a couple of teeth, which is enough when two things hold. A shoe on the rear cover keeps the film seated on the teeth, and a felt-washer clutch on the take-up spool slips once the sprocket locks, so a hard turn never loads the perforations.
